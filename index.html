Live Coaching 101

A short primer on using real-time data to raise performance. The goal is never “more numbers” — it's a faster, cleaner loop of observe → understand → adjust.

What to watch — and what it tells you

📍 Group & direction WHERE

The shot plot and clock-face direction show where shots are landing. A consistent bias (say, “8:00”) points to position, grip, sight or aiming — a fixable cause, not bad luck.

💯 Decimal & inner-tens QUALITY

Decimal scores and 10x counts reveal the quality of the hold. Two “tens” aren't equal — 10.1 vs 10.7 is the difference between surviving and dominating a final.

⏱️ Shot timing RHYTHM

The gap between shots exposes tempo. A shot fired far quicker than usual is often a rushed “grab” — frequently the low-scorer of the series.

🟡 Sighters vs match READINESS

Sighters confirm zero and warm-up quality. A big gap between sighter and match scores signals a focus or pressure issue, not a technical one.

📊 Series trend ENDURANCE

Score per 10-shot series shows stamina and concentration. A late-series drop points to fatigue or attention lapses to train for.

🔁 Shot-after-shot MINDSET

Watch what follows a great shot and a bad one. Over-holding after a miss, or relaxing after a ten, are mental patterns worth naming.

How to run a live session

1

Set up the athlete & session

Pick the shooter and discipline and start a session. Each one is saved, so you can compare today against last week.

2

Warm up on sighters

Use sighters to confirm zero and settle. Check the group is centred before match shots count — fix equipment issues here, not mid-match.

3

Observe more than you talk

Let the data accumulate. One or two shots is noise; a pattern across a series is signal. Resist coaching every shot.

4

One pattern, one cue

When a tendency is clear, give a single, specific, actionable cue (“finish your follow-through”) rather than a list. Then watch whether it lands.

5

Debrief with the summary

After the session, review series totals, inner-tens and trend together. Agree on one focus for next time — and track it.

Coaching tips

  • Coach the process, not the score. Talk about hold, trigger and follow-through; the score follows the process.
  • Let the athlete read the data first. Ask “what do you notice?” before you tell them — it builds self-coaching.
  • One change at a time. Stacking corrections muddies cause and effect; isolate variables.
  • Pressure-test on purpose. Use the live score to simulate finals tension, then watch rhythm and decimals hold or break.
  • Celebrate quality. A centred 10.6 under pressure deserves more attention than a lucky edge-ten.
